One test, every degree
The same 29 screens are shown to every UG student, whether they study computer science, BBA, biotech or
English. This is a design decision, not a shortcut, and it works because of what the questions measure.
The engine questions never test knowledge. They test reaction. Every scenario is built from universal student
raw material: money, teams, messages, ideas, broken processes, people struggling. “Sales are up 22%, profit
is down 8%, and nobody knows why” requires zero commerce knowledge. A CS student reads it as a
debugging problem. A BBA student reads it as a margin problem. Both readings produce the same honest
signal: did this mind lean in, or did its energy drop?
Degree enters the system at exactly three points, and nowhere else:

Worked example. A CS student and a BBA student give identical answers. Their radar is identical, their animal is
identical, their archetype is identical. Three things differ:
1. In C1, the CS student sees Technology & Data as the default card; the BBA student sees Business & Management.
If either picks their default while their reactions pointed elsewhere, the default-path flag fires for that student and
their report says so.
2. On the career cards, the same Data role renders as “Data Scientist, MSc Data Science” for the CS student and
“Data Analyst or Business Intelligence Analyst, MSc Business Analytics, open to non-CS backgrounds” for the BBA
student. Same fit %, different door.
3. The report's next-steps block cites their own degree when it recommends exposure: a project, an internship, one
conversation in the field.
Why not degree-specific questions? Three reasons. Comparability: one item bank means every student's
Hawk 10 means the same thing, which is what lets you norm the test later. Maintenance: one bank to fix,
translate and improve. Honesty: degree-flavoured questions leak expectations, and students answer as the
engineer or manager they think they should be, which is exactly the self-image trap this redesign removes.

The four metrics, defined and measured
Every question in the test exists to feed one of four metrics, and every metric surfaces as a named block in the
report. Nothing is asked that is not shown; nothing is shown that was not measured.
Metric 1: Personality. Who this student is when nobody is grading them. Measured by the seven slider items (A1
to A7), read in pairs, plus the primary animal. We never ask “are you a leader” or “what drives you”. We ask what
they actually did and let the pattern speak. Output: four strength sliders (Energy, Decision, Structure, Mobility)
each banded Clearly / Lean / Balanced, plus the animal pair and archetype.
Metric 2: Cognitive, reported as Approach. We do not run ability testing, so the report must never claim IQ or
aptitude. What we can measure honestly is thinking style: how this mind meets a problem. Sources: the
reaction pattern across B1 to B6 (instant solving vs wanting the answer vs energy drop), the Decision slider (A3,
A4), and resilience under the confirmation costs (D1, D2). Output: the Deliberate / Instinctive band, the
solve-first vs curious-passive pattern, and the honesty of the fit score itself.
Metric 3: Work Environment. The conditions under which this student does their best work. Sources: A5
(structure), A6 (drive without deadlines), A2 (solo vs team peak work), A7 (mobility), plus which role costs they
accepted in D1. Output: the environment paragraph in the report, phrased as “you will do your best work
where...”, and the mobility line that quietly gates the abroad module.
Metric 4: Career. The direction, the role inside it, and how ready they are. Sources: the six pull probes (B1 to B6)
building the radar, the domain choice (C1), the role resolution (C2 to C5), the confirmation pair (D1, D2), and the
reality items (F1 to F3). Output: the radar, the four career cards with fit %, and the next-steps block.

The one-way rule. Questions flow into metrics, metrics flow into report blocks, never the reverse. The student
must never be able to answer a question that directly writes its own report line. That is the flaw in the previous
set (asking “what role appeals to you” and reporting “we feel you should be a leader”), and it is the reason
verdicts now come only from combinations of three or more answers across different sections.

Scoring logic, step by step
Step 1. Radar. Each B item scores its own dimension: a = +3, b = +1, c = 0, d = -2. Convert to a 10-point scale:
dimension_score = (raw + 2) * 2 # a=10, b=6, c=4, d=0
This is what gives the radar real peaks and valleys. Delete any normalisation currently squeezing scores into the
7-to-8 band.
Step 2. Sliders. Each slider reads two items (Mobility reads one). Both answers on the same side = Clearly. One
= Lean. Split or both neutral = Balanced.

Step 3. Domain seeds for C1.
Finance = B1 + B4 Business = B2 + B5 Entrepreneurship = B4 + B3 Technology = B5 + B1 People & Society
= B6 + B2
Show four cards: seeded rank 1, seeded rank 2, the Q0 degree default, and the highest remaining. If the default
is already in the top two, the fourth card is the lowest-seeded domain as a wildcard. Set default_path_flag
when the student picks the default and it was outside the seeded top two.
Step 4. Role resolution. Sum role points across C2 to C5 using the weights printed with each option. Highest
total wins. If the top two are within 1 point, both go to the report as co-candidates and D1/D2 run on the higher
one.
Step 5. Confirmation. conf_total = D1 + D2, range -6 to +4. At +3 or more the role is confirmed. +1 to +2
provisional. 0 or less, the report leads with the mismatch, never the role.
Step 6. Fit %. See Section 7. Sort cards descending before render, always.
Step 7. Archetype. See Section 6. Thin-signal check first, flat check second, then pair the top two dimensions.
Deterministic: same answers, same archetype, every run.
Step 8. Flags. Evaluate all eight flags (Section 9). Growth tips render only from fired flags.
Step 9. Gate. Evaluate the abroad gate (Section 10) before rendering F4.

The animal system
Six animals, one per radar dimension. Chosen for the Indian context: every one of these is a compliment here.
The owl is deliberately excluded (ullu is an insult in most Indian languages), as are the fox and the snake. Animals
are shareable in a way colour systems are not, and PRISM already owns colours in this market.

The pair mechanic. The student's type is their top two radar dimensions. The higher dimension gives the
primary animal, the identity, rendered big and shareable. The second gives the modifier line. The archetype
name from Section 6 becomes the subtitle. Example header block:
YOUR TYPE: THE HAWK
with an Elephant's discipline
The Auditor. You see the hole in the plan before anyone else does, and you have the patience to fix the process that
caused it. Careful is not slow. Careful is your edge.
Rules. Primary = higher score; tie broken by C-branch alignment, then by the fixed priority Analytical, Practical,
People, Creative, Entrepreneurial, Leadership. No animal is ever awarded from thin data: an unearned Tiger
devalues every earned one. The two fallback states in Section 6 render without an animal headline.

Fit % and the career card engine
The formula (replaces whatever produced unsorted 73/86/80/68):
fit = role_component + radar_component + conf_component role_component = (role_score / 12) * 40 #
0..40 radar_component = (avg of the 2 radar dims mapped to this career / 10) * 30 # 0..30

Abroad gating logic
The word abroad must not appear anywhere in the test before F4, and F4 itself renders only when the student's
own earlier answers opened the door. Students who said they'd rather stay never see it and still receive a
complete report with a domestic next-steps block. On campus this matters: a test that goes blank for the
student staying in India tells that student, and any faculty member watching, exactly what the test is for.
show_abroad = (F3 in [Higher studies, Undecided]) AND (A7 in [a, c]) # global or conditional mobility
if E3 == d: show_abroad stays true but the family flag renders beside it if A7 in [b, d]: F4 and F5
never render; report closes with the domestic pathway F4 copy quotes A7 back: “You said you would move
anywhere in the world for the right opportunity. Would that include studying abroad?”
The quote-back framing converts better than a cold abroad question, and the students it excludes are the
ones a cold question would have alienated anyway.
